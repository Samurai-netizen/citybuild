| # | Decision | Alternatives considered | Why | Scope |

| 1 | The 64 producers' cycles end on all ten seconds of every ten (one producer placed a second), so 8 h hold 28,800 event times | All 64 in lockstep (2,880 event times) | Both do 184,320 completions (`04` §12), but each event time costs a scan of every building; spreading the ends is the most work a 10 s cycle can cause, so it is the true worst case | local |
| 2 | Content: 4 houses × 32 workers; 32 mines (no inputs, 2 ore, labor 20, 2 workers) and 32 smelters (1 ore → 1 ingot), alternating by id; ore capped at 10¹² (the content maximum), ingots uncapped; 100 ore at the start | Only uncapped items; separate chains per producer | The task asks for raw producers feeding the others and caps that are never reached; one capped and one uncapped output run both paths of the start rule. The mines make twice what the smelters take, and the starting ore covers the smelters before the first mine cycle ends, so nothing ever stalls | local |
| 3 | A 2-day research runs during both advances and completes in the 7-day one's dormant tail | No timers | `04` §12 says "plus timers" and "dormant time adds only timer events"; the tail must have one to process | local |

| 5 | The counts are literals with their derivation in a comment: 8 h → 2,880 completions and starts per producer; 7 d → 2,881 completions (the last cycle ends in the tail) and 2,880 starts; the research completes, every producer ends idle | Recompute them from the content in the test | Hand-checked numbers catch a wrong rule; formulas would repeat it | local |
| 6 | The timing test lands with the optimization, not in step 2's commit | Commit it failing (red) | Every checkpoint must be green (`16` §D); the numbers are recorded here | local |
