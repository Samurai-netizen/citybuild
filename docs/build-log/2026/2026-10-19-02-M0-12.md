  Steps 2 and 3 (sweep and line-by-line read of `State/`, `Rules/`, `Settlement/`, `IntegerMath`, and the content model for immutability and
  iteration order). Clean, with the evidence:
  - floats, `DateTime`, `TimeProvider`, `Random`, `Guid`, `Stopwatch`, `Console`, file I/O: none (grep).
  - statics: three, all immutable: `Simulator._eventSources` (a read-only list of stateless sources), `ContentIds.FormatDescription` (a
    string), `MutationResult.Ok` (an immutable object).
  - division: only in `IntegerMath` (`CeilDiv`, `ApplyPermilleReduction`) and the overflow-free midpoint of `CityState.TryGetBuilding`'s
    binary search, which is index arithmetic, not game logic (decision 4).
  - `Dictionary`/`HashSet`: used for lookups and duplicate checks only; nothing iterates one where order matters (content reads JSON maps
    through `OrderBy(key, Ordinal)`).
  - C# above 9: the compiler enforces it (`LangVersion 9.0`, netstandard2.1 and the `GuardUnityCompatibleBuild` target).
  - era- or research-specific `if`s, string id literals, number literals in `State/`, `Rules/`, `Settlement/`: none (grep).
  - error codes: every check of `04` §8 has its code, in the listed order, and `MutationErrorCode` has every engine code of `05`.
  - public setters: none on state or content (`private set` only; no `InternalsVisibleTo`). The `*Data` classes have setters by design: they
    are the JSON shape, and the engine never takes them.
  Findings:
  - **F1 (fixed): `Advance` allocates per event.** `foreach` over `IReadOnlyList<T>` boxes an enumerator (in `CityQueries`, and in
    `CityState.HasItems/AddItems/RemoveItems`), and every lookup in a `SortedDictionary<ItemId, long>` (the stock and the report totals)
    boxes the struct key on .NET 10 (measured: 24 B per `TryGetValue`, 0 B with a string key; its argument null check takes `object`). Eight
    hours of golden scenario #1 allocated 745,800 B against 3,944 B for two minutes.
  - **F2 (open): a timer due exactly at the clock completes after the starts.** `Advance` runs `StartEligible` before it looks at timers,
    which `04` §6 orders the other way round. A canonical city never has one (every timer ends ≥ 1 s after it is set), but a restored city
    can, and `04` §7 calls only a timer *before* the clock invalid.
  - **F3 (open): a restored producing building may have an end that its labor doesn't give.** The `Building` constructor checks the end is
    not before the segment, not that it equals `SegmentStartedAt + CeilDiv(LaborRemaining, AssignedWorkers)`; a restored 180-labor cycle
    could end in 1 s.
  - **F4 (open, hygiene): two subtractions on quantities and labor are not `checked`** (`Building.AssignWorkers`' remaining labor; the stock
    in `RemoveItems` is now checked as part of F1). Neither can overflow (both sides ≥ 0); the rule asks for `checked` anyway.
  Step 4, F1: regression test `Settlement/AdvanceAllocationTests` (eight hours of golden scenario #1 allocate no more than two minutes; it
  failed with 745,800 B against 3,944 B, then 325,320 B after the loops alone). Fix: index loops in `CityQueries` and `CityState`; a new
  internal `State/ItemTotals` (sorted parallel lists with binary search, only items above 0, an `IReadOnlyDictionary`) holds the stock and
  the report's totals (decisions 2 and 3). After it: 8 h and 2 min allocate the same. `CityStateTests.Lists_CastBack_CanNotBeChanged`
  changed its first assertion (decision 3); new `Inventory_ReadAsADictionary_GivesTheStockInItemOrder`. Simulation tests 686 → 688, 0
  warnings, format clean.
  F2: regression test `AdvanceTests.Advance_TimerDueAtTheClock_CompletesBeforeTheStarts` (a restored city whose `better_saws` ends at its
  clock; the camp's cycle must start with 30 labor, 15 s). It failed (40 labor, ends 1020). Fix: each pass of the loop completes what is due,
  then starts (decision 5); `04` §7's loop and its "timer at the clock" sentence say so, decision row 033 in `12`. Simulation tests 689.
